# Story Idea

**Working title:** The Keeper of Cape Vespers
**Genre:** YA survival thriller
**Protagonist:**
- Age: 22
- Gender: female
- Ethnicity: mixed Filipina-American
- Identity: A grieving photographer who takes a seasonal lighthouse-keeper job to honor her dead aunt
- Practical skill: lighthouse maintenance + medium-format film photography + carpentry + a private code her aunt taught her at twelve

**Setting:** Cape Vespers Island — a small automated lighthouse island in the Salish Sea, accessible only by a single weekly supply boat. One dock, one path, one tower, one cottage. When weather closes in, no boat can dock for 24-48 hours.

**Premise:** Iris Vaughn takes the relief-keeper job at Cape Vespers to grieve her aunt Marisol, who died on the island the previous winter — officially a fall. Five days in, Iris lifts a buckled kitchen floorboard and finds her aunt's hidden journal, written in the code Marisol taught Iris at twelve. The journal documents that someone — a man named Renner — has been using the lighthouse boathouse as a smuggling drop for forty years, and that the lighthouse inspector (Wren Delacroix) is on the payroll. The midpoint reveal is a moral fact: when Inspector Delacroix visits, she quotes a line from the hidden journal — proving she has read it and is complicit. As a storm closes the island on the same Thursday Renner is due, Iris must rig the long-decommissioned fog-signal whistle to broadcast the keeper's SOS pattern across the storm — the pattern her aunt taught her on the same rainy afternoon she taught her the code.

**Tone references:** The Lighthouse (2019) without the grotesque; The Vanishing; Lean on Pete with more fog.

**Target runtime minutes:** 15

**Antagonist preference:** Two antagonists — Wren Delacroix (female lighthouse-service inspector, complicit, with followable logic) and Otis Renner (male retired-fisherman smuggler, the killer).

**Narration voice preference:** female, controlled, expressive (tongtong)

**Visual style preference:** Pacific Northwest painterly cinematic with cold blue-grey palette and warm amber lamp accents. Andrew Wyeth tempera feel crossed with cold-climate drama film.
