# Task 05 — More images per scene, richer editing (not stale)

**Issue title:** T05 — Editing: more shots per scene, dynamic cutting

**Source:** task-main.md — "The images used are too less ,the editing done per scene is tooo stale."

## Root cause

The v1 has 74 shots across 22 scenes = ~3.4 shots per scene. That's the bare minimum (1 wide + 1 medium + 1 close). A cinematic short film needs 5-8 shots per scene for proper coverage.

More critically: the editing is stale because shots are static (no motion) and cut directly from one to the next (no crossfades). Each shot stays on screen for its entire narration segment duration (~15s), which feels slow.

## What needs to happen

1. Expand the shot list from 74 to ~150-180 shots (5-8 per scene). Add:
   - Inserts (extreme close-ups of key props: the journal, the crank handle, the contact sheet, the knot on the hasp).
   - Reaction shots (Iris's face reacting to what she's reading/seeing).
   - Detail shots (the lens rotating, the lamp gear, the dock cleats, the wood stove ticking).
   - Establishing shot → coverage → close-up pattern for each scene.
2. Cut on action — when the narration says "she climbed the tower," cut to a shot of her feet on the iron stair, then to her hand on the railing, then to her face at the top.
3. Vary shot duration: 4-8s for close-ups and reaction shots; 8-15s for establishing shots and emotional moments. Don't let any single shot exceed 15s.
4. Add crossfade transitions between shots within a scene (0.3-0.5s).
5. Add hard cuts at scene boundaries (act transitions).

## Acceptance

- 150-180 shots total (vs 74 in v1).
- Shot durations vary (4-15s).
- Crossfades between shots within scenes.
- Hard cuts at scene boundaries.
- A "rhythm check" script verifies the average shot duration is 6-10s (not 15s).
