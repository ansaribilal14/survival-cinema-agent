# Comparative Analysis — Three Reference Works

| Dimension | Pym (A) | Scarlet Plague (B) | Moreau (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arena | Open ocean → Antarctic | Coastal CA, 60 years post-collapse | Closed tropical island |
| Threat | Environment + mutiny + unknown | Disease + social collapse + forgetting | Mad science + Beast Folk + moral horror |
| Time-scale | Weeks to months | One afternoon + 60-year flashback | Months |
| Protagonist's first job | Hide | Remember | Observe |
| Midpoint | Ocean → polar exploration | Personal → cultural transmission | Physical → moral survival |
| Resolution | Truncated, open | Open, elegiac | Closed but misanthropic |

All three replace one survival arena with another at midpoint. None stacks the same threat twice.

## Agency curve
Each protagonist: passive/reactive in act 1, proactive in act 2, decisive in act 3. Our pilot follows this.

## Suspense
All three use withheld information as primary device. Pym locked in dark hold (visual deprivation). Scarlet Plague withholds meaning of "chauffeur." Moreau withholds Beast Folk nature. All use small wrong details early that pay off as central horror later. All use betrayal through kindness.

## Midpoint reveal
- Pym: setting change (structural).
- Scarlet Plague: thematic change (death → forgetting).
- Moreau: moral fact (Beast Folk are surgically-altered animals).

**Strongest pattern: Moreau's — midpoint reveal is not a new monster; it is a fact that retroactively re-reads every earlier scene as sinister. We will use this.**

## Endings
- Pym: truncated, no resolution (modern audiences reject).
- Scarlet Plague: open, elegiac (acceptable for short story, but pilot wants stronger closure).
- Moreau: protagonist permanently changed, choosing solitude. Arc closes; world does not.

**Our pilot: close protagonist's arc while leaving larger world ambiguous.**

## Female presence
None of the three references has strong female protagonist. This is the gap the directive asks us to fill. We will not gender-swap any plot. We will design a protagonist whose femininity is a story asset, not a costume — young woman whose practical skill is specifically credible for her age and gender, whose weakness is specifically credible, whose relationships with other women are multiple and varied.

## Synthesis: what we take, what we leave

**Take:**
- Escalation-ladder architecture (Pym)
- Frame-narrative voice (Scarlet Plague)
- Midpoint-as-moral-fact technique (Moreau)
- Sensory deprivation as cheap cinematic set-piece (Pym)
- Single-exit geography (Moreau, Scarlet Plague)
- Antagonist whose logic we can almost follow (Moreau)
- Betrayal through kindness (all three)

**Leave:**
- Pym's open ending
- Scarlet Plague's all-male cast and passive present frame
- Moreau's specific Beast Folk concept
- Victorian convention of fainting as scene transition
- Any specific character identity, setting, or plot

## The original IP we will build
A young woman, early 20s, with specific practical skill, stranded in closed environment (one exit). Hosts appear kind. Midpoint reveal is a moral fact about hosts. She must act decisively to escape. Arc closes with permanent change in identity.

Setting, antagonist, specific mystery invented fresh.
