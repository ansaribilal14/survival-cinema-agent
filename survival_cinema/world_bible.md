# World Bible — Cape Vespers

## Geography
- **Cape Vespers Island**: ~1.5 km × 0.5 km uninhabited island in the Salish Sea, off the Olympic Peninsula coast of Washington State.
- **The Lighthouse**: 70-foot white-and-black cast-iron lighthouse, built 1897, automated 1984. First-order Fresnel lens (original 1897), no longer turning on the original clockwork; now a 12-volt LED beacon on a photocell.
- **The Cottage**: two-room wooden cottage, 30 m from the tower. Wood stove, gas lamps, hand-pump well, shortwave, an old enlarger for developing film, a kitchen chair with a broken cane.
- **The Boathouse**: corrugated-iron shed at the head of the dock. Holds a 14-foot aluminum skiff with a broken fuel line, coils of rope, an old tarp. Under the tarp is Renner's drop crate.
- **The Dock**: wooden, with steel cleats. The single exit.
- **The Path**: 250-meter gravel-and-boardwalk path from dock to cottage/tower through low brush (salal, Oregon grape, sword fern) and wind-stunted Douglas fir. South end has a tiny graveyard with two headstones (1898, 1903).

## Climate and weather
- Mid-September through mid-October (24 days).
- Cool (10–18 °C), foggy mornings, clear afternoons, occasional storm fronts.
- One significant storm on the climactic Thursday.
- Semi-diurnal tides, ~2.5 m range.

## The secret history
The lighthouse was automated in 1984. Since then, four keepers have died on the island, all ruled accidental:
- 1995 — heart attack on the tower stair
- 2007 — drowned from the dock
- 2014 — fall on the path (Marisol's predecessor)
- 2025 — fall on the path (Marisol)

Marisol's notebook documents that none of these were accidents. Renner killed all four. Wren Delacroix, the inspector, has been paid by the smuggling operation since 1995 and covered all four deaths.

## The smuggling operation
- Operator: Otis Renner, retired fisherman out of Neah Bay.
- Route: cheap black-market cigarettes and small arms from Oregon to Vancouver Island, via the Cape Vespers boathouse as a drop point.
- Drop cadence: every Thursday, weather permitting, at four in the morning.
- History: Renner inherited the route from his father, who used the boathouse as a drop in the 1970s.
- Inspector cut: Wren Delacroix has been paid since 1995.

## The Petrel (Halen's boat)
- 32-foot wooden-hulled supply boat built in 1968, rebuilt engine 2019.
- Diesel engine, single screw, top speed 9 knots.
- Route: Cape Vespers (Thursdays), Biddle Light (Tuesdays), Smith Island (Wednesdays), New Dungeness (Fridays).

## The Coast Guard
- Stationed in Port Angeles.
- 47-foot motor lifeboat that can run in any weather.
- Summoned by shortwave on Channel 16 or by the keeper's SOS whistle pattern (long-short-long-short-long).

## Continuity details (for image generation)
- Daymark: tower white with one black band in the middle.
- Lens: brass-and-glass Fresnel, six prismatic panels around a central bullseye.
- Lamp rotation: clockwise (viewed from above), once every 15 seconds.
- Cottage exterior: weathered grey shingles, white window trim, green roof.
- Halen's boat: white hull with green trim, name "PETREL" in green letters on the stern.
- Renner's boat: old aluminum skiff with a small gasoline outboard, dull red hull.

## Time period
Present day (2026), but the island's technology has not changed since 1984. Iris has a digital camera but rarely uses it; her aunt's Yashica is the camera the film sees her with. No cell service.

## Tone and atmosphere
Salt-bleached wood, cold blue water, warm amber lamp light, deep green fir. Mostly silent except for wind, surf, the lamp's gear rotation, the wood stove ticking, the shortwave's crackle, rain on corrugated iron. One original piano theme (three chords) used once in act 1 and once in the coda.
