# Story Bible — *The Keeper of Cape Vespers*

**Original IP.** Not derived from any reference work. See `originality_audit.md`.

---

## Logline

A grieving 22-year-old woman takes a seasonal job as the relief keeper of an automated lighthouse on a remote island where her aunt died the previous winter. While she came to find solitude, she finds her aunt's hidden journals — and learns that the woman who raised her was being silenced by someone who came to the island every Thursday.

## Genre

Young-adult survival thriller with gothic-island atmosphere. Female-centric. Cinematic voice-over narration over AI-generated imagery. Runtime target: 17–19 minutes.

## Audience

Long-form YouTube viewers who enjoy survival thrillers, strong female leads, and contained-location suspense. Age targeting: 16+.

## Setting

Cape Vespers Island — a small (~1.5 km × 0.5 km) uninhabited island in the Salish Sea, off the Olympic Peninsula coast of Washington State. One dock, one path, one tower, one cottage. The single exit is the spine of the suspense.

## Characters

- **Iris Vaughn** (protagonist, 22, mixed Filipina-American) — photographer and relief lighthouse keeper. Claustrophobia from a childhood trauma; grief-fog from her aunt's death.
- **Aunt Marisol Vaughn** (deceased at 51) — previous relief keeper, US Coast Guard veteran. Drives the plot via her coded journal.
- **Halen Yoo** (28, Korean-American) — supply-boat captain. Ambivalent ally; knew about the smuggling but was silent out of fear.
- **Wren Delacroix** (40, white) — lighthouse-service regional inspector. Antagonist, revealed at midpoint. Has her own followable logic.
- **Otis Renner** (53, white) — the Visitor, retired fisherman and smuggler. The killer. His logic: "The route fed my family for forty years. The service took the lighthouse and broke the deal. I was restoring the deal."
- **Ed Bose** (70) — retired keeper on the mainland, voice on the shortwave. Mentor.

## Plot (five beats)

### Act 1 (Scenes 1–5): Arrival
Iris arrives on the *Petrel* at dawn, takes the tower, finds her aunt's coat, hears a boat engine in the fog at 3am.

### Act 2A (Scenes 6–10): Settling and wrongness
Daily routine; finds the hidden metal box under the floorboard; decodes her aunt's journal; learns someone comes on Thursdays; photographs a fisherman's loop knot on the boathouse hasp.

### Act 2B (Scenes 11–15): The reveal
Develops the negative — has a photograph of Renner. Inspector Wren Delacroix visits. **MIDPOINT REVEAL:** Wren quotes a line from the hidden journal — proving she has read it and is complicit.

### Act 3A (Scenes 16–19): Iris becomes proactive
Reads the third journal entry naming Renner; breaks into the boathouse, photographs the smuggling crate; confesses to Halen; reads the bookmarked keeper's manual page on the fog-signal whistle.

### Act 3B / Climax (Scenes 20–21): Thursday
The storm breaks. Renner arrives. Iris hides in the lamp room. She rigs the long-decommissioned fog-signal whistle with the brass crank handle to broadcast the keeper's SOS pattern (long-short-long-short-long). Halen hears it and returns; the Coast Guard is summoned.

### Act 4 / Resolution (Scene 22): After
Dawn. Renner confesses on the dock. Wren arrested off-screen. Iris leaves the island permanently. Coda: a postcard arrives at Bose's six months later from Iris's new life in maritime archaeology.

## Voice and tone

Narration by Iris, present-tense voice-over, addressed (silently) to her dead aunt Marisol. Sparse, specific, cold-warm. The island is cold; the women are warm; the men are cold.

## Visual style

Pacific Northwest painterly cinematic. Cold blue-grey ocean; warm amber lamp light; salt-bleached wood; deep green fir. 16:9 cinematic with a slight vignette and fine film grain. The lamp rotation sweep (every 15 seconds) is the visual heartbeat of the film.

## Music, ambience, SFX

One original piano theme (three chords), used once in act 1 and once in the coda. Otherwise: wind, surf, distant foghorn, the lamp's gear rotation, the cottage's wood stove ticking, rain on corrugated iron. The climactic fog-signal whistle.
